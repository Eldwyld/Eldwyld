the forager's oath

Ethics of the Wild Harvest

The first rule of the hedge: the land is not a shelf.
Take as a guest, not as an owner.

Be certain, then be certain again. Identify by at least three features and one trusted guide before anything touches a basket — and never trust an app alone with your life.

Take a tithe, not a harvest. One in ten, never the first found, never the last standing. Leave the grandmother plants to seed the next hundred years.

Know whose land you stand on — legally and historically. Ask permission where permission is needed; tread lightly where it is given.

Leave protected species be. Learn your region's at-risk list and let rarity be a boundary, not a trophy.

Harvest clean. Skip roadsides, sprayed verges, dog-walked strips and downstream of runoff. The plant remembers where it grew.

Do no harm getting there. Stay on durable ground, close the gates, fill your holes, pack out more than you brought.

Give something back. Water, a scattered seed, a cleared tangle of litter — the old bargain, kept small and often.

My own rules — added as the land teaches them
